### Week 1 — Deine Erste Woche — Your First Week: Meeting People

#### Day 1 — Greetings & Introducing Yourself (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → listening → quiz → speaking → quickwin

**Grammar**
You do not need grammar terms to start speaking. Use these four building blocks: say hello, say your name, ask a name, and answer "How are you?".
Steps:
1. Say hello any time: "Hallo!"
2. Say your name: "Ich heiße ..." (I am called ...) or "Ich bin ..." (I am ...).
3. Ask a friend their name: "Wie heißt du?" To be polite, ask "Wie heißen Sie?"
4. Answer "Wie geht's?" with "Gut, danke. Und dir?" (Fine, thanks. And you?)
Examples:
- **Hallo, ich heiße Lea.** — Hello, I'm Lea.
- **Ich bin Tom.** — I'm Tom.
- **Wie heißt du?** — What's your name?
- **Gut, danke. Und dir?** — Fine, thanks. And you?
Note: Mnemonic: Hallo is hello with an "a" — the easiest possible start. When in doubt, smile and say "Hallo".

**Vocabulary**
| German | English | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| Hallo | Hello | HAH-loh |
| Guten Morgen | Good morning | GOO-ten MOR-gen |
| Guten Tag | Good day / Hello | GOO-ten TAHK |
| Guten Abend | Good evening | GOO-ten AH-bent |
| Tschüss | Bye | choos |
| Auf Wiedersehen | Goodbye | owf VEE-der-zayn |
| Wie geht's? | How are you? | vee gayts |
| Gut, danke | Fine, thanks | goot DAHN-keh |

#### Day 2 — Where You Are From: Ich komme aus… (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → listening → matching → speaking → quickwin

**Grammar**
"Ich komme aus ..." means "I come from ...". Copy this shape and you can name any country or city. The question is "Woher kommst du?" (Where are you from?) — notice "kommst" is the same word with a "t" when you ask someone else.
Steps:
1. Say your country: "Ich komme aus Deutschland."
2. Say your city: "Ich komme aus Berlin."
3. Ask a friend: "Woher kommst du?"
4. Answer "aus" the same way both times — you never change this word.
Examples:
- **Ich komme aus Indien.** — I'm from India.
- **Ich komme aus Spanien.** — I'm from Spain.
- **Woher kommst du?** — Where are you from?
- **Ich komme aus der Türkei.** — I'm from Turkey.
Note: Remember the shape: Ich komme aus + place. Once you have this, "Woher kommst du?" is the only question you need to keep the talk going.

#### Day 3 — Your Family: Meine Familie (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → matching → fillblank → quiz → quickwin

**Grammar**
Copy these phrases: "Das ist meine Mutter" (this is my mother) and "Das ist mein Vater" (this is my father). Notice the little word before the person: "meine" for women like Mutter and Schwester, "mein" for men like Vater and Bruder. Do not memorize a rule yet — just notice it when you hear it.
Steps:
1. Point and name: "Das ist meine Mutter."
2. Name your father: "Das ist mein Vater."
3. Say your sister's name: "Meine Schwester heißt ..."
4. Notice the pattern: women take "meine", men take "mein".
Examples:
- **Das ist meine Mutter.** — This is my mother.
- **Das ist mein Vater.** — This is my father.
- **Meine Schwester heißt Lea.** — My sister is called Lea.
- **Mein Bruder ist zehn.** — My brother is ten.
Note: Reference card (no need to memorize yet): "meine" goes with women, "mein" with men. You will use both every day from now on.

#### Day 4 — Things Around You: Was ist das? (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → flashcards → fillblank → quiz → quickwin

**Grammar**
Every thing in German comes with a little word: "der", "die", or "das". Do not try to learn the rule — learn each new thing together with its little word, like one piece (der Tisch, die Lampe, das Buch). To ask what something is, say "Was ist das?" and answer "Das ist ...".
Steps:
1. Point at something and ask: "Was ist das?"
2. Answer for the things you know: "Das ist ein Tisch."
3. "ein" is used with der/das things, "eine" with die things.
4. Learn every new thing with its little word from day one.
Examples:
- **Was ist das?** — What is that?
- **Das ist ein Tisch.** — That is a table.
- **Das ist eine Lampe.** — That is a lamp.
- **Das ist ein Buch.** — That is a book.
Note: Reference card: things never come alone — each one brings der, die or das. "ein/eine" means "a/an" and follows the same little word pattern.

#### Day 5 — What You Do: Ich heiße, ich wohne, ich spreche (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → fillblank → scramble → quiz → quickwin

**Grammar**
Action words are easy once you meet them in phrases. Notice how the ending changes when you switch from "I" to "you": "Ich heiße" (I'm called) becomes "Du heißt"; "Ich wohne" (I live) becomes "Du wohnst"; "Ich komme" becomes "Du kommst". Do not learn a table — learn each pair as you use it.
Steps:
1. Introduce yourself with an action: "Ich wohne in Berlin."
2. Ask the other person: "Wo wohnst du?"
3. Talk about languages: "Ich spreche Englisch und Deutsch."
4. Notice: ich ends in "-e/or nothing", du ends in "-st/-t".
Examples:
- **Ich wohne in Berlin.** — I live in Berlin.
- **Wo wohnst du?** — Where do you live?
- **Ich spreche Deutsch.** — I speak German.
- **Sprichst du Englisch?** — Do you speak English?
Note: Reference card: "ich" → "du" usually means changing the ending to "-st" or "-t". You will pick up the exceptions (like heiße → heißt) by hearing them.

#### Day 6 — Your Age: Zahlen & Wie alt bist du? (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → listening → fillblank → quiz → quickwin

**Grammar**
To say your age: "Ich bin ___ Jahre alt" (I am ___ years old). To ask: "Wie alt bist du?" (How old are you?). You only need the numbers from one to twelve for this, so start there — eleven (elf) and twelve (zwölf) are the only two that do not follow any pattern.
Steps:
1. Count on your fingers: eins, zwei, drei, vier, fünf, sechs.
2. Continue: sieben, acht, neun, zehn, elf, zwölf.
3. Say your age: "Ich bin zehn Jahre alt."
4. Ask back: "Und du? Wie alt bist du?"
Examples:
- **Ich bin zehn Jahre alt.** — I am ten years old.
- **Wie alt bist du?** — How old are you?
- **eins, zwei, drei** — 1, 2, 3
- **elf, zwölf** — 11, 12
Note: Reference card: age uses "bin" (I am) + number + "Jahre alt". The numbers 1–12 are special — learn them as one song.

### Week 2 — Das Alphabet — The Alphabet

#### Day 1 — The German Alphabet (6 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → flashcards → listening → quiz → speaking → quickwin

**Grammar**
German letter names sound different from English. Do not memorize the whole alphabet at once — start by spelling your own name.
Steps:
1. A sounds like "ah", B like "beh", C like "tseh".
2. W is "veh", V is "fow", J is "yot", Z is "tsett".
3. Ä sounds like "eh" with your mouth open. Ö sounds like "eh" with rounded lips. Ü sounds like "ee" with rounded lips.
4. ß is called Eszett and sounds like a sharp "s".
Examples:
- **Anna = A-N-N-A** — ah – enn – enn – ah
- **Tom = T-O-M** — teh – oh – emm
- **Lea = L-E-A** — ell – eh – ah
- **Maus = M-A-U-S** — emm – ah – oo – ess
Note: Mnemonic: Say your name in slow motion, letter by letter. That turns the alphabet into something personal.

**Flashcards**
- **W** → veh — *Wasser (water)*
- **V** → fow — *Vater (father)*
- **J** → yot — *Ja (yes)*
- **Z** → tsett — *Zeit (time)*
- **Ä** → eh — *Äpfel (apples)*
- **Ö** → round lips, eh — *schön (beautiful)*
- **Ü** → round lips, ee — *über (over)*
- **ß** → sharp s — *Straße (street)*
- **CH** → kh after a/o/u; sh after e/i/ä/ö/ü — *Bach / ich*

### Week 3 — Zahlen 0–12 — Numbers 0–12

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → listening → quiz → speaking → quickwin

**Grammar**
Numbers are useful for time, prices, and age. Zero to twelve are special — learn them like a short song.
Steps:
1. Hold up your fingers as you say eins, zwei, drei.
2. A clock face helps: zwölf is at the top.
3. A die has one to six dots: eins through sechs.
Examples:
- **null, eins, zwei, drei** — 0, 1, 2, 3
- **vier, fünf, sechs** — 4, 5, 6
- **sieben, acht, neun, zehn** — 7, 8, 9, 10

### Week 10 — Aussprache — Pronunciation Deep-Dive

#### Day 1 — Vowel Combinations (5 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → vocabulary → listening → quiz → quickwin

**Grammar**
When two vowels sit together, they often make one sound. Learn the sound, not the grammar name.
Steps:
1. ie = long "ee" sound: sie, lieben, vier, sieben.
2. ei = "eye" sound: zwei, mein, klein, Eis.
3. au = "ow" sound: Auto, laut, Haus, auch.
4. eu / äu = "oy" sound: neu, teuer, Freund, Bäume.
5. vowel + h = hold the vowel longer: gehen, fahren, sehen, zahlen.
6. double vowels = hold longer: Haare, leer, Boot.

#### Day 3 — Accusative Case & Separable Verbs (3 tasks)

**Task sequence:** grammar → grammar → quiz

**Grammar**
The accusative case is used for direct objects. Only masculine articles change (der -> den).
Examples:
- **Ich sehe den Mann.** — I see the man. (der -> den)
- **Ich habe ein Buch.** — I have a book. (no change for ein)
- **Er trinkt den Kaffee.** — He drinks the coffee. (der -> den)
Note: der -> den, ein -> einen (masculine only). Everything else stays the same.

**Grammar**
The prefix goes to the end of the main clause.
Examples:
- **Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf.** — I get up at 7. (aufstehen -> auf...)
- **Wir fahren nach Berlin.** — We drive to Berlin. (fahren -> no split)
- **Er ruft seine Mutter an.** — He calls his mother. (anrufen -> an...)
Note: Common separable prefixes: ab-, an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-
